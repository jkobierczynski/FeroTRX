# Y.1564 service activation

`ferotrx y1564` tests services through a network the way ITU-T Y.1564
describes, to show that each service gets the bandwidth profile and the
quality it was sold with before it goes live. FeroTRX sends on one
interface into the network and receives on another where the network
delivers the frames, with a signature in every frame
([loss and latency](loss-latency.md)), so loss, delay and delay variation
are measured one way.

```sh
sudo ferotrx y1564 examples/y1564.toml -i eth1 --rx eth2 --report y1564.json
```

It writes `y1564.json` and `y1564.txt`, prints a line per step as it ends,
and exits with an error when a step fails, so a script or CI job can act
on it.

## Services

A service is a class of traffic with a bandwidth profile and acceptance
criteria (SAC):

* **CIR**, the committed information rate: frames within it are *green*
  and must meet the acceptance criteria.
* **EIR**, the excess information rate on top: these frames are *yellow*,
  and the network may drop them.
* **CBS** and **EBS**, the burst sizes the policer allows, in bytes.
* **Acceptance criteria**: the frame loss ratio (FLR) of the green frames,
  their mean one-way frame transfer delay (FTD), their frame delay variation
  (FDV, the 99th percentile of the delay minus its minimum), and the
  availability in the performance test.

Green and yellow frames are separate flows, each with its own signature,
so they are counted apart. A network that drops yellow frames first (a
*color-aware* one) must be able to tell them apart: give yellow frames
their own description, with another DSCP or VLAN priority. A service whose
yellow frames are like its green ones is *color blind*: the network can
only police the total, so in the policing and EBS steps the green frames'
loss is reported but not judged, only the rate. `color_aware` sets this
either way.

Rates count Ethernet frames with their FCS (layer 2), as MEF bandwidth
profiles do; `layer1 = true` counts the preamble and the gap too. Frame
sizes are without the FCS, as everywhere in FeroTRX: `sizes = 252` sends
256-byte frames on the wire.

## The tests

**Service configuration test**, one service at a time:

| Step | Sends | Passes when |
|---|---|---|
| `25% CIR` … `100% CIR` (`steps`) | green frames at that part of the CIR | the green frames meet the SAC |
| `CIR+EIR` (when there is an EIR) | green at the CIR, yellow at the EIR | the green frames meet the SAC, and what arrives is at least the CIR and at most CIR + EIR |
| `policing` | green at the CIR, yellow at 125% of the EIR (`overshoot`), or 25% of the CIR without an EIR | what arrives is at most CIR + EIR (a policer is there and works) and at least the CIR; the green frames meet the SAC when the service is color aware |
| `CBS burst` (when `cbs` is set) | green frames in bursts of CBS bytes, as fast as the port sends, averaging the CIR | the green frames meet the SAC |
| `EBS burst` (when `ebs` and an EIR are set) | green at the CIR, yellow in bursts of EBS bytes averaging the EIR | the green frames meet the SAC (color aware), or what arrives is at least the CIR |

The rate checks allow `ir_tolerance` (5%) either way. Yellow frames may
always be lost; how many is reported.

**Service performance test**: every service at once, green frames at its
CIR, for `performance_duration` (15 minutes by default; Y.1564 suggests
up to 24 hours for a final acceptance). Per service: FLR, FTD, FDV, the
information rate, and loss and mean and maximum delay per `interval`
(1 second). An interval losing more than `ses_flr` (50%) is severely
errored (SES); as in ITU-T Y.1563, ten of them in a row make the service
unavailable from the first of them, and ten good ones in a row make it
available again from the first of those. Availability is the share of
available intervals; it must be at least the service's `availability`.

Frames sent in an interval are counted from the send time in their
signature, so a frame delayed into the next second still counts for the
second it was sent in. What was sent per interval comes from the
sequence numbers, which only advance on frames that left: the first
number that arrived from one interval and from the next tell how many
were sent in between. So a second in which the sender fell behind
counts what it sent, not loss.

The sender never catches up in a burst. When it falls more than 5 ms
behind its schedule (a busy CPU, a paused VM), it leaves out the frames it
missed instead of sending them back to back, which a policer would rightly
drop and count against the network. A step that left out more than 3% of
its frames says "the sender fell short"; its FLR counts only frames sent.

## The profile

```toml
format = 1

[y1564]
line_rate = "1gbps"             # the link's speed
tests = ["configuration", "performance"]
steps = [25, 50, 75, 100]       # configuration steps, percent of the CIR
step_duration = "60s"
overshoot = 125                 # policing step: yellow at 125% of the EIR
burst_tests = true              # CBS and EBS steps, for services that give them
performance_duration = "15m"
interval = "1s"
ir_tolerance = 5                # percent
ses_flr = 50                    # percent
unavailable_after = 10          # intervals
layer1 = false
max_time = "2h"                 # stop after this long; the report is partial
rx_wait = "500ms"               # time for frames in flight after each step

[[service]]
name = "data"
packet = "eth(src=02:00:00:00:01:02,dst=02:00:00:00:01:01)/ipv4(src=10.9.1.2,dst=10.9.2.3,dscp=10)/udp(sport=4000,dport=9000)"
yellow_packet = "eth(src=02:00:00:00:01:02,dst=02:00:00:00:01:01)/ipv4(src=10.9.1.2,dst=10.9.2.3,dscp=12)/udp(sport=4000,dport=9000)"
vary = ["port_src=4000..4063"]  # field engine variables, as in streams
sizes = "emix:abceg"            # EMIX letters, or 508, "imix", "60:7,590:4,1514:1"
cir = "100mbps"                 # or a number of Mbit/s
eir = "50mbps"
cbs = 12176                     # bytes
ebs = 12176
flr = 0.1                       # percent
ftd = "20ms"
fdv = "5ms"
availability = 99.9             # percent
# color_aware = true            # default: true when yellow frames differ

[output]                        # as in traffic profiles: backend, workers, ...
```

`packet` (or `packets = [...]`, sent in turn) uses the syntax of
[`ferotrx build`](packets.md); the payload is cut or padded to each size.
Frames start with `eth`, addressed to the network's first hop. EMIX
letters are Y.1564's: `a` 64, `b` 128, `c` 256, `d` 512, `e` 1024, `f`
1280 and `g` 1518 bytes on the wire, each letter one frame of the
sequence. Each service's CIR and EIR must fit the line rate, and for the
performance test all CIRs together must.

`examples/y1564.toml` has two services, voice and data. `ferotrx check`
reads a Y.1564 profile and warns when a service with an EIR has yellow
frames like its green ones.

## On the command line

```text
ferotrx y1564 PROFILE -i IFACE --rx IFACE
    [--tests configuration,performance] [--service NAME]...
    [--line-rate RATE] [--step-duration TIME] [--performance-duration TIME]
    [-B pcap|af_xdp|dpdk] [--workers N] [--report FILE] [-q]
```

The options replace the profile's values; `--service` tests only the
services named. Ctrl-C stops the step under way, and the report is partial.

```text
configuration 100% CIR: data offered 10.000 Mbit/s, IR 9.989 Mbit/s, FLR 0.00%, FTD 15.0us, FDV 52.6us: PASS
configuration CIR+EIR: data offered 15.000 Mbit/s, IR 14.983 Mbit/s, FLR 0.00%, FTD 76.0us, FDV 1.11ms, yellow lost 0.00%: PASS
configuration policing: data offered 16.250 Mbit/s, IR 15.107 Mbit/s, FLR 0.00%, FTD 10.8us, FDV 61.8us, yellow lost 18.47%: PASS
...
Service performance test (10s, intervals of 1s):
  service        IR Mb/s       FLR       FTD         FDV   available     SES  result
  voice            4.000     0.00%     8.1us      54.3us    100.000%       0  PASS
  data             9.986     0.00%   106.5us      2.75ms    100.000%       0  PASS
```

The JSON report (`"format": "ferotrx-y1564-1"`) has the run's metadata
(`info`: host, interfaces, backend, the profile), the configuration steps
with every flow (offered and sent rate, frames sent and received, loss,
latency figures) and every service's verdict and reasons, the performance
test with its intervals, and a verdict per service.

## Elsewhere

* **Scenarios**: a `y1564` check runs a profile file or an inline table on
  the check's `iface` and `rx`; `expect = "pass"` (the default) needs every
  step to pass, `expect = "fail"` needs one to fail. Metrics: `passed`,
  `steps`, `steps_failed`, `services_failed`, `flr_pct`, `ftd`, `fdv`
  (worst over the steps), `availability_pct` (worst service), `ses`,
  `ir_mbps` (performance test) and `yellow_loss_pct`. See
  [scenarios](scenarios.md).
* **Control server**: `y1564.start`, `y1564.get`, `y1564.stop`,
  `y1564.list` and `y1564.remove` ([remote control](remote-control.md)).
* **Python**: `Client.y1564_start(...)`, `y1564_get`, `y1564_wait`,
  `y1564_stop` ([the Python client](python-client.md)).
* **GUI**: the Y.1564 tab ([the desktop GUI](gui.md#y1564)).

## Lab script

`scripts/y1564-netns.sh` builds a router in a network namespace that
polices two services by DSCP: voice (color blind, CIR 4 Mbit/s) and data
(color aware, CIR 10 and EIR 5 Mbit/s, green and yellow policed apart),
with tc `police` actions on its ingress, or htb classes with short queues
when the kernel has no `act_police`. The test passes; then, with data's
green policer at 70% of its CIR, it must fail at data's 100% step while
voice still passes. CI runs it.

```sh
sudo scripts/y1564-netns.sh
sudo STEP=10s PERF=1m scripts/y1564-netns.sh
```

## Not done

* Tests whose ends are on two machines need their clocks in step for the
  delay (NTP or PTP), as [loss and latency](loss-latency.md) explains;
  loss and rates do not.
* A loopback at the far end (frames sent back by a reflector) is not
  supported: the receiving interface must see the frames as sent.
